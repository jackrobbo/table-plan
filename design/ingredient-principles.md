# Ingredient principles (draft, 6 Nov 2026, for Jack to edit)

Menus come first, then the ingredient list is locked, then each supermarket is matched. See `design/ingredient-availability.md` for how the current 116 ingredients fared across Tesco, Sainsbury's and Asda.

1. **Generic first.** Build recipes from everyday ingredients every big supermarket stocks (chicken breast, semi-skimmed milk, tinned tomatoes). Of today's 116, 70 have a match at all three shops; most of the rest are branded or unusual.
2. **Branded items only on purpose.** A named brand (a protein granola, a peri-peri rub, whey isolate, protein pops) exists at one shop only. Allow one only when it is central to the dish or the macros, and mark it so we can offer a generic swap at other stores.
3. **Own-brand equivalents are fine.** The match tool already accepts "own-brand equivalent". Premium or organic lines are a different product, not a match for a standard one, unless we choose them as the quality tier.
4. **Say what quality means.** Decide once: standard, "better" (for example free range, British, higher-welfare, named ranges) or both, and apply it per ingredient. (Jack to define: what does "better quality products" mean for the first three supermarkets?)
5. **Plain produce is a pack or a weight, not a brand.** Loose items (carrots, bananas, onions, potatoes, peppers) are priced per kg or per each, so match by the form we want (loose, bag, or each) and state it in the ingredient's unit.
6. **Pack size must work with the portion.** Prefer ingredients where a normal pack is close to what the recipe uses, so cost per meal isn't distorted (a 4x145g tuna pack for one recipe is a poor fit).
7. **Seasonal or patchy lines are risks.** Avoid ingredients that are often out of stock or only stocked in some seasons or stores, unless there is a standard fallback.
8. **Cupboard staples are matched too.** Oil, rice, pasta, spices and stock need a priced product per store (the 33 staples have no Morrisons product today, only a fixed price). Decide whether they are priced per use or per pack.
9. **One ingredient, one slug.** Reuse existing ingredients across recipes before adding a new one. Every new ingredient costs a Morrisons link and a match at each store.
10. **Every ingredient has a CoFID code and allergens** before a recipe using it goes live (the importer already enforces this).
